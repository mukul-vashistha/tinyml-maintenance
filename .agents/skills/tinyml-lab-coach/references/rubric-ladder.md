# Rubric ladder

Use the level for the current phase. Do not apply the final challenge standard to a beginner in Phase 00.

## Level 1: orientation, Phase 00

The learner can explain:

- what the system predicts;
- why advance warning is useful;
- which actions the estimate may support;
- where the problem is written;
- one simple connection from problem to code or result.

The coach may supply most of the structure and write the record. Catching an AI mistake is optional.

## Level 2: guided understanding, Phases 01 and 02

The learner can distinguish:

- prediction time, target, horizon, evidence, cost, and action;
- current or historical information from future or hidden information;
- one row from a machine history;
- a past-only rolling feature from a future label.

The coach explains concepts and guides file inspection. The learner supplies the paraphrase and leakage reasoning.

## Level 3: guided interpretation, Phases 03 and 04

The learner can:

- connect an observation to a next experiment;
- explain why class imbalance changes metric choice;
- distinguish ranking metrics from threshold behavior;
- support an interpretation with a saved result.

The coach may draft the record after the learner interprets the evidence.

## Level 4: engineering judgment, Phases 05 and 06

The learner can:

- make a prediction before seeing a result;
- preserve and revise a failed hypothesis;
- defend a split from the deployment question;
- explain why final test evidence cannot select a model;
- distinguish the questions and limits of supervised and unsupervised methods;
- identify a material AI claim that needs correction or qualification.

The learner should provide most of the reasoning. The coach edits and challenges it.

## Level 5: operating judgment, Phases 07 and 08

The learner can:

- separate raw score, calibrated probability, threshold, and action;
- explain the jobs of calibration, policy-selection, and test evidence;
- evaluate ranking and threshold behavior under shift;
- decide when automation lacks support;
- state the remaining risk and next experiment.

The saved entry should read like a concise engineering handoff.

## Level 6: independent extension

The learner owns repository inspection, blueprint, scope, tests, controlled experiment, AI review, and handoff. The coach may question and review but must not create the initial engineering judgment on the learner's behalf.
